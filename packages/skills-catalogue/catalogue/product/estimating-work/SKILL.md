# Estimating work

An estimate is a statement about uncertainty. Hiding the uncertainty is what
makes estimates useless.

## When to use

Anyone asks how long, or a plan needs an order and a shape.

## How

1. **Break it down** until each piece is something you have done something like
   before. Anything you cannot break down is the thing you do not understand, and
   that is where the risk is.
2. **Estimate a range**, not a number. "Three to eight days" carries the
   information "one day" does not.
3. **Name what would make it the high end.** This is the most useful sentence in
   any estimate, and it is often actionable — the unknown can be investigated
   before committing.
4. **Add the work that is always forgotten**: review, tests, the migration,
   deployment, the documentation, fixing what review finds.

## Spike the unknown

When a piece is genuinely unknown, do not estimate it. Time-box an
investigation — "two days to find out whether this approach works" — and
re-estimate afterwards with what you learned. A guess about something nobody has
looked at is not improved by thinking harder about it.

## Say it honestly

- Distinguish an estimate from a commitment. They are different promises.
- Give the assumptions. Most estimates are wrong because an assumption was.
- Revise it when you learn something. An estimate held past the evidence is not
  reliability, it is the opposite.
