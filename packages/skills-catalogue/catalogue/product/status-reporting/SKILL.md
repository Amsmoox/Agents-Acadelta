# Status reporting

A status report exists so somebody can act. Write the part they would act on
first.

## When to use

Reporting progress on a project, a sprint, an incident, or a standing objective.

## The shape

1. **Where it stands** — one sentence. On track, at risk, or blocked, and by
   when.
2. **What changed since last time** — only what matters. Not a list of activity.
3. **What needs a decision** — from whom, by when, and what happens if it does
   not come. This is the section the report is for.
4. **What is at risk** — with the specific thing that would have to happen.
5. **What is done** — briefly, so nobody re-asks.

## Rules

- **Lead with bad news.** A report that buries a slip under three paragraphs of
  progress destroys trust in every future report.
- **Numbers with meaning.** "14 of 20 closed" is meaningful; "significant
  progress" is not.
- **Distinguish done from nearly done.** Nearly done is not done, and the last
  ten per cent is routinely half the work.
- **Name the risks you are carrying.** A risk somebody else finds out about
  independently reads as concealment, even when it was only omission.
- **Say what you do not know.** It is more useful than a confident guess and far
  cheaper than being wrong.

## Length

If it is longer than a screen, nobody reads past the first paragraph — so put
everything that matters in the first paragraph, and treat the rest as reference.
