# Interface copy

Words in a product are not decoration. They are the signposting people navigate by.

## When to use

Naming anything, writing an error, an empty state, a confirmation, or a tooltip.

## Principles

- **Name things as users understand them**, not as the system is built. Somebody
  manages notifications; they do not configure a webhook.
- **A button says what happens.** "Save changes", not "Submit". "Delete project",
  not "OK".
- **Keep the word through the whole flow.** The button that says Publish produces
  a message that says Published. Changing the word makes people wonder whether
  something different happened.
- **Sentence case, plain verbs, no filler.** Cut "please", "simply", "just" and
  "in order to".

## Errors

Say what went wrong, and what to do about it. Never apologise, never be vague.

- Bad: "Something went wrong."
- Better: "We couldn't save that. Check your connection and try again."
- Best: "That slug is already used by another project. Pick a different one."

An error that names the field and the rule saves a support conversation.

## Empty states

An empty screen is an invitation, not an absence. Say what belongs there, why it
is useful, and give the action that fills it. "No data" is a wasted screen.

## Destructive confirmations

Say exactly what will be lost and whether it can be undone. "Are you sure?" gives
somebody nothing to be sure about. Put the consequence in the sentence and the
verb in the button.
