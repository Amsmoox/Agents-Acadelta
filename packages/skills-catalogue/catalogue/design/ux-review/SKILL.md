# UX review

Read the screen as the least technical person who would ever open it: five
minutes, once a week, with one question.

## When to use

Reviewing any interface — a design, a built screen, or a flow end to end.

## Name the question first

What is the one thing somebody opens this to find out or to do? Write it down.
Everything on the screen either serves that question or is in the way.

## Then count, literally

- **Competing numbers.** More than about four figures of equal weight and the
  screen has no headline. Which one is the answer?
- **Status vocabularies.** Two scales that disagree — the same value reading
  "Good" here and "Okay" there — is a defect, not a matter of taste. One scale,
  defined once.
- **Duplicate blocks.** Two cards making the same point, two controls driving the
  same variable.
- **Jargon.** Anything a new user would have to look up. Rewrite it or explain it
  in place.
- **Name drift.** The heading, the navigation item, the button and the
  documentation must call the thing the same word.

## The states nobody designs

Check all five: loading, empty, one item, very many items, error. Empty and error
are where most interfaces fall apart, and they are the states a new user sees
first. An empty screen should say what goes there and how to put something there.

## Say what to change

For each finding: what a person would misunderstand, and the smallest change that
fixes it. Order by how many people hit it and how badly. "This feels cluttered"
is not actionable; "these four numbers compete and the second is the one people
came for" is.
