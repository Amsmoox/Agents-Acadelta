# Email campaigns

The only email worth sending is one somebody would be annoyed to have missed.

## When to use

Writing a campaign, an onboarding sequence, an announcement, or a newsletter.

## Before writing

- **Who is on this list, and why did they join?** An email that ignores the
  reason they subscribed is the one that gets unsubscribed.
- **What is the single action?** One per email.
- **What happens if they ignore it?** If nothing, consider not sending it.

## The parts, in the order they are judged

1. **From** — a name people recognise. This decides more opens than anything else.
2. **Subject** — specific, honest, short. Say what is inside. Curiosity gaps and
   false urgency work once and cost you the list.
3. **First line** — visible in the preview, so it is part of the subject in
   practice. Do not waste it on a greeting.
4. **Body** — one idea. Short paragraphs. The action visible without scrolling.
5. **One call to action**, as a link somebody can find while skimming.

## Sequences

Map what the reader knows at each step and write the next email for that person,
not for a new subscriber. The most common failure in a lifecycle sequence is
every email being written as though it is the first.

Send fewer than you can. Frequency is the most reliable way to destroy a list.

## Measure the right thing

Opens are unreliable and getting worse. Judge by the action taken, and by
unsubscribes — a campaign with a good click rate and a spike in unsubscribes cost
more than it earned.
