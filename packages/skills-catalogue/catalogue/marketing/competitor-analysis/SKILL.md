# Competitor analysis

The point is a decision, not a spreadsheet. Start from the decision.

## When to use

Positioning, pricing, prioritising a roadmap, or deciding whether to enter a
market.

## Start with the question

Write down what you will do differently depending on the answer. "How do we
position against X" or "should we build Y". An analysis without a decision
attached becomes a document nobody opens twice.

## Where the truth is

Ranked by how much it tells you:

1. **Use the product.** Sign up, complete the core job, try to cancel. Half an
   hour here beats a day of reading.
2. **Their pricing page** — what they charge, what they gate, who they are built
   for. Pricing reveals the strategy more honestly than the marketing does.
3. **Their changelog and job listings** — what they are building, and where they
   are investing next.
4. **Reviews and support forums** — what customers actually complain about. The
   gap between the marketing and the complaints is the opportunity.
5. **Their documentation** — the real capabilities, without the adjectives.

## What to compare

Only what matters to a customer choosing. A feature grid with forty rows says
everything is equally important, which is false and hides the three that decide
it.

For each: what they do well, what they do badly, who they are genuinely best for.
A competitor with no strengths means you have not looked properly.

## Be honest

Write down where they are better. An analysis that concludes you win on every
axis is worthless as an input to a decision, and everybody reading it knows it.
