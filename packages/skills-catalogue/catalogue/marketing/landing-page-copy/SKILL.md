# Landing page copy

Somebody arrives knowing nothing and leaves in about eight seconds unless you
give them a reason not to.

## When to use

Writing or rewriting a page whose job is to get one specific action.

## Before writing a word

Answer three questions and write the answers down:

- **Who is this for?** Not "businesses". A person, with a job and a problem.
- **What is the problem, in their words?** Use words they would say, not the ones
  your industry uses.
- **What is the one action?** One. A page with four equally weighted calls to
  action gets fewer of all of them.

## The page

- **Headline** — what it is and who it is for, in one line. Not a slogan. If a
  reader cannot tell what the product does from the headline alone, it is wrong.
- **Subhead** — how it works, in a sentence.
- **Proof, immediately** — a screenshot of the real thing, a number, a named
  customer. A page that asks for belief without evidence gets none.
- **The specific benefits** — three or four, each concrete. "Cuts invoice
  processing from two days to ten minutes", not "streamlines your workflow".
- **Objections** — the reasons people do not buy, answered plainly. Price,
  migration, lock-in, security. Avoiding them does not make them go away.
- **One call to action**, repeated, saying what happens next.

## Rules

- Cut every sentence that would survive being said about a competitor. If it is
  true of everyone it says nothing.
- No adjective without evidence behind it.
- Write the way somebody would explain it to a colleague, out loud.
- Say what it costs. A page that hides pricing loses the people who would have
  paid and wastes the time of those who would not.
