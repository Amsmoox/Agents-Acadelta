# Metrics and dashboards

A dashboard nobody acts on is a cost with no benefit.

## When to use

Defining what to measure, or building a view somebody will look at regularly.

## Choosing a metric

- **Tie it to a decision.** If no plausible value would change what anybody does,
  do not track it on a dashboard. Put it in a report nobody has to look at.
- **Prefer rates and ratios to totals.** Totals go up because time passes.
- **Pair every metric with its counterweight.** Speed with quality, growth with
  retention, volume with cost. A single metric optimised alone is always gamed,
  usually unintentionally.
- **Beware vanity.** Page views, signups, raw counts. They rise reliably and mean
  little on their own.

## Define it once

Write the definition down: what is counted, what is excluded, which timezone,
which timestamp. Then use that definition everywhere. Two dashboards showing
different numbers for the same word is how people stop believing all of them.

## Building the view

- **One question per screen.** Name it in the title.
- **One headline number**, large, with the comparison that gives it meaning —
  against last period, against target. A number alone is not information.
- **Show the trend**, not just today. A point tells you nothing about direction.
- **Four figures at most** competing for attention. Past that there is no headline
  and the eye has nowhere to land.
- **Label what "good" is.** A reader who cannot tell whether 68 is good learns
  nothing from being shown it.

## Maintain it

Review dashboards periodically and delete the panels nobody looks at. An
abandoned dashboard is worse than none, because someone will eventually trust it.
